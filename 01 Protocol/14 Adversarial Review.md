# 14 Adversarial Review

Attack the conclusion before anyone else does. This is a distinct stage, not a mindset applied to the previous one.

## Write the opposing case in full

Not a caveats paragraph — the actual argument, written as if by someone who believes it and is good at their job. It should be uncomfortable to read.

If the opposing case cannot be written convincingly, one of two things is true: the conclusion is genuinely strong, or the research never seriously looked. Check the evidence log for disconfirming entries. If there are none, that is the answer, and the research is not finished.

## The checklist

Run all of it. Log anything that survives.

**Structure**
- What single fact, if false, collapses the conclusion?
- Which claims are single-sourced? Does the argument rest on any of them?
- Is any inference presented in the grammar of an observation?

**Evidence**
- Base rate: how often is the thing being predicted true in general? Is this case argued to be an exception, and on what grounds?
- Selection: did the sources survive because they are right, or because they are visible? Who publishes on this and why?
- Recency: is one recent, vivid data point carrying weight that a longer series would not support?
- Vintage: is anything load-bearing now stale?

**Mechanism**
- Is a correlation being described as a cause? What is the proposed mechanism, and is it evidenced?
- Would the same evidence be produced by a completely different explanation?

**Framing**
- Did the conclusion move at all during the research, or does it match the opening prior? If it never moved, why not?
- Would a domain expert wince at anything here? At what specifically?
- Is any of this true but irrelevant to the decision in the brief?

## What happens to holes

Holes are not patched with softer wording. Each one goes to:

- `04 Open Threads.md` — if it is researchable and remains open
- The deliverable's limitations section — if it is irreducible
- `05 Decision Log.md` — if it changes the conclusion, with the reasoning

A hole that gets neither resolved nor stated is a defect, and it is the specific defect that destroys trust in research when it surfaces later.

## Revision is allowed and expected

If the review breaks the conclusion, the conclusion changes. Append the reversal to `05 Decision Log.md` with its date and cause. A reversed conclusion is the system working, and a research record that contains one is more credible than one that does not.
