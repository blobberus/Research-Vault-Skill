# 32 Uncertainty and Calibration

## Confidence bands

Use these words, with these meanings. Consistency is the point — a reader must be able to compare confidence across findings and across projects.

| Term | Rough probability | Warrants |
|---|---|---|
| **Established** | >95% | Multiple independent A-tier sources, no credible dissent |
| **High confidence** | 80–95% | Strong sourcing, minor gaps, opposing case is weak |
| **Moderate confidence** | 55–80% | Reasonable evidence, real gaps, opposing case is coherent |
| **Toss-up** | 45–55% | Genuine equipoise. Say so — do not pick a side for the sake of a conclusion |
| **Unlikely / unsupported** | <45% | Evidence points the other way, or nothing supports it |
| **Unknown** | — | Searched for, not found. Not the same as unlikely |

**Unknown is not a low probability.** Conflating "we could not find out" with "probably not" is the most consequential confidence error in research, and it is very common.

## Say what the confidence rests on

A band without a reason is decoration:

- ✗ "Moderate confidence the moat is durable."
- ✓ "Moderate confidence — margin and renewal data are strong (E-004, E-011), but no competitor win-loss data was available (E-021), so displacement risk is unmeasured."

## Precision

- A derived figure carries no more significant digits than its weakest input. Two-significant-figure inputs give a two-significant-figure answer.
- Prefer a range with its basis to a point estimate with none: "$40–60M, from the disclosed segment split against headcount" beats "$50M".
- False precision reads as rigour and is the opposite. `$47.3M` from rounded inputs is a claim to knowledge that does not exist.
- Percentages: state the base. "Up 40%" from what, over what period.

## Hedge words are not calibration

"Somewhat", "arguably", "it seems", "could be" express discomfort, not probability. Replace each with a band and a reason, or delete it. If a sentence needs three hedges, the underlying claim is `unknown` and should be labelled so.

## Calibrate against base rates

Before assigning high confidence to a prediction, ask how often this class of thing turns out true in general. A specific, vivid, well-argued case is not evidence that it beats the base rate — it is usually evidence that the argument is well-constructed.
