# 33 Writing Standards

Applies to deliverables only. Project notes are exempt and should stay rough.

## Structure

- **Answer first.** The first paragraph states the conclusion and its confidence. Not the background, not the method, not what was done.
- **Then the case**, strongest evidence first.
- **Then the counter-case**, stated fairly enough that someone who holds it would recognise it.
- **Then the gaps.**

Length is set by the decision, not by the effort spent. A two-week project can have a one-page output. Padding a short answer to justify long work is a serious failure — it hides the answer.

## Sentences

- Plain words. "Use" not "utilise", "about" not "with respect to", "because" not "due to the fact that".
- Active voice, named actors. "The company restated 2024 revenue" — not "revenue was restated".
- No throat-clearing. Delete "It is important to note that", "In today's rapidly evolving landscape", "This report will examine".
- One idea per sentence.

## Numbers

Every number carries a source and a date. Every derived number shows its method inline or in a footnote. Every percentage names its base. No number appears in a conclusion that does not appear, sourced, in the body.

## Honesty markers

- Inference is labelled: *"Nothing states this directly; it follows from…"*
- Single-sourced claims say so.
- Stale figures carry their vintage: *"as of Q2 2026"*.
- What was not established gets its own section. It is never left out to make the piece look complete.

## What not to do

- Do not use confidence language loosely — the bands in `03 Standards/32 Uncertainty and Calibration.md` are the vocabulary.
- Do not describe the research process except where it bears on the reliability of a finding. Nobody wants the tour.
- Do not summarise the summary. One statement of the conclusion.
- Do not soften a finding to be agreeable. If the answer is unwelcome, say it plainly and once.
- Do not present a recommendation where the brief asked for an assessment.
